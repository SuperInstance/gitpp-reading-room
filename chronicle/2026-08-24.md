## (e) What actually happened on 2026-08-24

Here is the part that changes the story.

**No repository was lost.** I checked all fifty-nine backups against the live
account. **Fifty-eight originals still resolve independently** - `mist-game`
(65,574 KB), `hermes-cloudflare` (63,703 KB), `elephant` (46,929 KB),
`fleet-jepa-midi` (19,791 KB), `zeroclaw-dissertation` (1,958 KB),
`base60-lattice`, `batten-spline`, `dual-band-guard`, `platos-shell` and forty-eight
more. The fifty-ninth, `fishinglog-ai`, resolves only through its own rename
redirect. **Nothing was recovered because nothing needed to be.**

**Thirty-one of the fifty-nine copies are empty** - 0 KB. The complete list:

```
ACE-Step-1.5              collective-unconscious   fleet-tts          openrooms
Constraint-Theory         dual-band-guard          forgemaster-shell  plainsong-mcp
base60-lattice            elephant                 hermes-cloudflare  plainsong-worker
batten-spline             emergence-engine         hermes-perception  platos-shell
fleet-dashboard           fleet-envelope           hermes-reader      roblox-testkit
fleet-radio               officers-quarters        scrap-spark        screen-agent
scummvm-arcade             silence-map              smp-notebook       spatial-registry
superinstance-design-system  the-relay              wesleys-imagination
```

Two of those originals are enormous and perfectly healthy:
`hermes-cloudflare` is **63,703 KB**, `platos-shell` is **4,336 KB**. The script
enumerated a repository list and cloned it; for thirty-one names the clone
produced nothing, and the run continued regardless.

**So: what the evidence supports.**

Between **17:43 and 19:43 local on 2026-08-24**, a script walked an
alphabetical list of the account's repositories and pushed a copy of each to a
new repository named `recovered-copy-20260824-<original-name>`. Sixty repos, two
hours, unbroken id sequence, no operator, no commit, no PR, no issue, no
description, no topics, no published package. Thirty-one copies came out empty.
`rc-20260824-01` - a byte-exact copy of `fleet-static-host`, a repository that
was never at risk - was created in the middle of the sweep under a different
name.

The name says *"recovered"*. The evidence says nothing was recovered. The most
economical reading of a sixty-repo, two-hour, mostly-empty, undocumented bulk
copy performed while every original was intact is **a precaution that was never
needed** - insurance bought on a night when the fleet looked more fragile than it
was, and left behind, unlabelled, when the morning showed it was not.

**What I could not determine.**

1. **Why.** No record of a trigger exists anywhere I searched. I cannot say
   whether someone believed data was at risk, whether a script was misfiring, or
   whether this was a rehearsal.
2. **Who or what ran it.** The repositories are unattributed; no service account
   appears on them; the events archive no longer reaches the date.
3. **What `fishinglog-ai` actually lost.** It is the only repository that
   appears to have been deleted, and its recovery stops at 2026-04-07. Whether
   it was worked on in the intervening four months is unknowable from here.
4. **Why `rc-20260824-01` was named differently** from the other fifty-nine,
   and why `operational-fiction` was created 2h21m before the rest with an id
   84,000 lower.
5. **The brief's 216-file / 43,676,142-byte figure** for the
   `fleet-static-host` / `rc-20260824-01` pair. `public/mist/` reproduces
   exactly; the whole-tree figure does not match any branch I measured.

**What I recommend against.** Nothing here is a defect. Sixty repos left behind
by a precaution that turned out to be unnecessary is a fossil of a night's
judgement, and the fifty-eight originals that still stand are the proof that the
judgement, whatever it was, cost the fleet nothing. The history is the point.
Keep it.

### Appendix - how these numbers were obtained

- Repos cloned into `/tmp/hist/cl/` with `git clone --filter=blob:none`; all
  comparisons are on `ls-tree -r -l` output (mode, blob sha, size), not on
  working-tree bytes.
- Creation times from `GET /repos/SuperInstance/{name}`; ids are globally unique
  and monotonic, so their ordering is independent evidence of creation order.
- The eight named in the brief were cloned and diffed individually; the other
  fifty-one were characterised from the API.
- **Read-only throughout.** Nothing was created, pushed, renamed or deleted.
